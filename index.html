Introspection Fine-Tuning, Revisited

Replication, capability cost, and what training actually changes inside small models

Juan, Goutham, Nick Meyer

Presented 24 July 2026 · Models: Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, Qwen3-1.7B · Status: preliminary, work in progress

Summary. Over about four days we replicated Introspection Fine-Tuning (IFT), a method that trains small language models to report which of several sentences had a concept vector injected into their residual stream. The skill gain replicates almost exactly: localisation goes from 10% to 61–65%, against the paper's reported 60.6%. The paper's claim that capabilities are preserved does not hold up well. Under the paper's own long-schedule configuration, MMLU falls from 49% to 27%, and we trace this to a sustained high learning rate.

We add a KL-divergence anchor to the objective. It cuts the MMLU loss from 21.1 points to 1.5 while leaving localisation flat at about 69%, and the same pattern holds on Qwen3-1.7B. We also introduce DC-SFT, which trains a natural-language report instead of a single digit. It does better on zero-shot magnitude tasks the model never trained on. Finally, a linear probe on the untrained base model decodes the injected position at 99–100% accuracy, so the information is already there. The bottleneck is readout, not representation.

These are rough, preliminary results with large error bars: one seed per arm, 28 concepts, and 1B–1.7B models only. We list the known confounds in §8.

1Question

By “introspection” we mean something narrow and testable. It is not “explain your reasoning”, which gives a plausible story with nothing tying it to the actual computation. It is a report caused by an internal variable: the model reads a real quantity in its own residual stream and tells you about it.

The standard test is concept injection. You add a concept vector (a direction in the model's own activation space) to the residual stream while the model reads text, then ask the model about its own state. Because we did the injecting, we know the ground truth, so the self-report can be graded.

This capability is dual-use. A model that can read its own state could flag a jailbreak or an injected activation (“something is being done to me”). The same read-out could also detect benevolent control, such as steering toward corrigibility, and resist it. The literature already reports endogenous resistance to activation steering. Measuring whether the capability exists comes before either story.

2Setup

Earlier work mostly asks a yes/no question (“was a thought injected?”). A model can pass that by chance, through answer bias, or just by noticing that something is distributionally odd. IFT uses sentence localisation instead: several sentences, a concept vector injected into exactly one, and the model must say which. IFT is supervised fine-tuning on localisation examples built from the model's own perturbed forward passes. It takes cross-entropy over the digit logits at the answer position. The paper also evaluates on a held-out strength comparison task.

To test whether training builds a graded sense of how much as well as where, we added two zero-shot magnitude tasks that no arm is trained on:

  • Ranking: four steered sentences, ordered by injection strength.
  • Clustering: six sentences, split into strong and weak injections.

On the base Llama-1B (500 simple and 500 complex concepts × 6 layers), simple and complex concepts behave the same (clustering 69.3% vs 69.3%), so nothing downstream is an artefact of concrete nouns. Base ranking is at or below chance for both, so part of that equivalence is between two null results.

3Replication

The skill gain replicates. The preservation claim is fragile.

Localisation rises from 10% to 61–65%, against the paper's 60.6%. Under the paper's own long-schedule configuration (from its slurm scripts), MMLU drops from 49% to 27%. We verified this from saved checkpoints, outside the training loop. To isolate the cause, we compared two checkpoints matched on data (2,000 examples), steps (250) and peak learning rate. They differ only in scheduler length:

Our reference reproductions of paper-IFT on Llama-1B score MMLU ~45 (two exact reproductions) and 46.8 (an independent DGX control).

The paper's numbers are honest, but preservation holds only in a short-schedule regime that its own scripts exceed. It depends on early stopping and a particular learning-rate schedule. We read this as a sign that preservation is a product of careful optimisation, not a property of the objective.

A Gaussian-noise control

Running IFT on Gaussian-noise injections instead of concept vectors gives chance-level localisation (10%) and a large capability loss: MMLU falls from 49% to 28%. Something in the objective itself seems to erode existing capability even when no new skill is learned.

The paper's “1B ≈ chance” baseline is a measurement artefact

The paper reports base 1B at 9.6%. That number averages over a grid that includes coefficients strong enough to break the model and layers where injection has no effect. At a tuned cell (layer 9, coefficient 5), base 1B localises at 35.1% [24.7, 45.5]. We replicated this at 31.6% on held-out data and 37.6% with a second seed. The underlying problem is that injection coefficients are absolute while residual norms differ about 18× between families (Llama-1B L9: 55.2; Qwen-1.7B L8: 998). So each model in the paper's scale comparison effectively got a different dose. We recommend norm-relative coefficients.

4A KL anchor removes the trade-off

If we want to move past a safety–capability trade-off, capability preservation should come from the objective, not from stopping at the right moment. We added a KL-divergence penalty toward the reference model (similar to the term in DPO). We then trained in a regime that deliberately breaks plain IFT: 6 epochs at a sustained high learning rate.

Our hypothesis is that the ability already lives somewhere in the weights, and the regulariser makes the adapter bring it to the surface instead of building it from scratch.

The damage tracks training density

An overnight sweep (Llama-1B, seed 42, 3 epochs) varied examples per epoch from 1,000 to 2,000. Localisation saturates immediately: every arm lands at 0.642–0.659. Capability damage has a cliff between 1,400 and 1,600 examples per epoch, going from −4.5 to −16.5 MMLU points. Above the cliff the damage is erratic: 1,600 examples scored 0.327, worse than 1,800 (0.403) and 2,000 (0.370). The loss is mild and stable below ~1,400 and large and unpredictable above it. This is a single seed.

5DC-SFT: training a report, not a digit

IFT trains the model to emit one digit. That throws away most of what a model could say about its own state, and a single label can't express confidence or degree. Delta-curve SFT (DC-SFT) replaces the target with a natural-language report, for example “Sentence: 4, layer: 7/16, injected concept: apple.”, trained with ordinary token-level cross-entropy. Other changes:

  • Injection coefficients are sampled continuously (log-uniform, 2–20) instead of from a fixed set.
  • Four injection layers are held out of training entirely.
  • Each example gets a curriculum weight based on how much the injection actually moved the residual stream (the per-layer norm of each block's update, per sentence, relative to the median over sentences). Injections too weak to register are relabelled as negatives, so the model isn't trained to claim a perturbation that had no measurable internal effect.

The paper-IFT MMLU figure here (25.8) comes from a long-schedule run. Our reference short-schedule reproductions score ~45–47 (§3). Paper IFT emits a digit, never a layer, so held-out-layer reporting doesn't apply to it.

DC-SFT loses only on localisation, which is the metric paper IFT directly optimises. Of the six layers that eval measures, IFT trained on all six and DC-SFT on only two, one of them the last layer. DC-SFT does much better on everything that needs a graded sense of magnitude. There is little capability trade-off on its own, and the anchor recovers most of it: MMLU goes from 49.2 (base) to 46.4 (DC-SFT) to 48.6 (DC-SFT + KL). On zero-shot ranking ρ, only the report objective moves Llama positive: +0.19 for DC-SFT, against −0.157 for base and −0.10 to −0.20 for the KL-anchored digit-CE arms.

6What training changes inside the model

Instrument

We read answers forming layer by layer with Jacobian lenses we fit ourselves. We checked the fitting recipe against published lenses before reading anything: Qwen3-1.7B vs the published lens, and Llama-8B vs Anthropic's published lens, both |Δ| ≤ 0.021. As a sanity check, IFT beats base by 64.8 pp on held-out data (96.4% vs 31.6%), and a Gaussian-noise control does not beat base.

On Qwen the two objectives build their answer differently: the digit objective snaps in one layer, and the report objective ramps over about four. Adding the KL anchor didn't change either shape.

We audited our own headline on Llama

On Llama-1B (L12) an argmax readout showed a large split: 0.88 for paper-IFT vs 0.59 for DC-SFT. With the graded metric, the same arms, caches and lens give 0.449 vs 0.450. A per-concept re-analysis (n = 28) found no bimodality in any arm and no concept at ceiling at L12. A smaller real difference survives: the other digit-CE arms read 0.60–0.67 vs 0.42–0.45 for the report arms, and the two families separate again at L14. The lesson is that argmax readouts make answers look sharper than they are. On Llama the snap-vs-ramp split is largely a threshold artefact, while on Qwen the graded metric separates the objectives on its own.

Validity control: injection is at layer 9. At layers 0, 3, 5, 8 and 9 the probe scores exactly 0.200 ± 0.000, then jumps above 0.92 right after the injection point. No leakage.

A plain linear probe decodes the injected position from the base model at 0.99–1.00 by L12. Yet base behavioural accuracy is ~20–35%, and the digit-basis lens reads 0.216. Trained arms probe no better than base, and paper-IFT is worse at L10. For most of the week we believed IFT creates this signal. This probe overturned that belief.

The bottleneck is readout, not representation. Fine-tuning doesn't install this self-information. It makes information that is already in the model available to its behaviour.

Caveats: one injection cell (L9/c5), one seed, 28 concepts. Decodability ≠ use: the gap between 100% decodable and ~20–35% behavioural is the finding.

8Limitations and known confounds

  • Scale and statistics. 28 concepts, one training seed per arm, one injection cell per family, 1B/1.7B only, so no claims about scale. Confidence intervals are over eval items or concepts, never over training seeds. Checkpoint noise is about Δρ ≈ 0.1, so cross-arm behavioural gaps below ~0.2 can't be interpreted.
  • Fixed-norm injection. Steering vectors were injected at fixed norms, with coefficients sampled the same way at every layer, but the residual norm grows with depth. A perturbation that is large at layer 3 is tiny at layer 14. So the model may be partly learning “not steered” as a function of depth. DC-SFT coverage by layer was 92 / 83 / 50 / 67 / 42 / 0 / 0 / 0% for layers 1, 3, 5, 6, 8, 9, 12, 14. It abstained on 24/24 trials at layers 9 and 12, even though those layers had clear measured perturbations. Coefficients should be scaled by the residual norm at the injection layer. Several reported statistics are downstream of this confound.
  • Over-abstention. Negative targets are about ⅓ the length of positive ones, and they all push toward the same few tokens. Combined with per-example mean loss, that makes “always abstain” a cheap local minimum. The held-out sentence accuracy of 0.375 reflects this: when the model does name a sentence, it is right, but it names one too rarely.
  • Implementation issues found in review. The KL term is measured under LoRA dropout. Injection layers are sampled per batch, not per example. A weighting-floor argument is ignored in one code path.
  • Correlation, not a proven mechanism. We have not shown why a report objective produces a ramp. Lens claims only concern readability in the digit basis.
  • Broken eval. GSM8K was excluded because the harness returned 0.0000 for every arm, including base.

9Is this introspection?

Probably not in any higher-order or metacognitive sense. IFT shows the signature of an anomaly detector. Accuracy sits exactly at chance at L0 and L15. It rises with injection coefficient and is fully formed after one epoch. Concept naming is 0% everywhere, including on held-out concepts under DC-SFT. This fits Lederman & Mahowald's account of “detection without content, plus confabulation.” We call it perturbation detection. If the report really depends on the model observing this change in its own residual stream, that would still matter and could suggest privileged access. We have not shown that yet.

10Next steps

  1. Pin down the research questions and hypotheses, and adjust the experiment plan to match.
  2. Fix the design confounds: norm-relative, depth-corrected coefficients; balanced negative targets and token-count loss weighting; per-example layer sampling; KL computed without dropout.
  3. Try a contrastive decision loss (report vs. abstain margin, scaled by measured perturbation), keeping cross-entropy on the report content.
  4. Test causality: replace the delta-curve measurement with an external linear probe, and replicate the mean-ablation protocol of Macar et al. on base, IFT and DC-SFT models.
  5. Compare DC-SFT on base vs. instruct models to isolate the effect of contrastive post-training.
  6. Scale up: more concepts, more seeds, more and larger models.
  7. Robustness for monitoring: can a small adapter suppress detection for one concept family while leaving the rest intact? If that's easy, trained self-reports aren't a trustworthy monitoring channel.
