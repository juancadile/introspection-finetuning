Problems for Language Model Introspection

Juan Cadile, University of Rochester, Department of Philosophy · jcadile@ur.rochester.edu · juancadile.com
Nick Meyer, Columbia University, Department of Computer Science · nick.meyer@columbia.edu · nickmeyer.xyz

Claims about LM introspection concern distinct capacities with different evidential demands. Current evidence partly supports first-order state sensitivity, but no study has yet demonstrated metacognitive representation.

The problem

Language models (LMs) can produce reports about their own internal states, that much is clear. Whether these reports surface from a process we’d call introspection is less clear, and contested.

Part of the problem is conceptual imprecision: different papers use the same term for different capacities, or different terms for the same capacity. Consequently, results presented as supporting or challenging “introspection” often bear on different claims.

Here, we attempt to make this more clear by: 1) defining introspection for language models, 2) operationalizing its study into one framework, and 3) evaluating existing empirical claims under this framework.

One self-report, four causal routes

Ascribing introspection: capacities and criteria

We adapt four evidential criteria from Lindsey (2025): accuracy, grounding, internality, and metacognitive representation. Rows are capacities discussed in the literature under the label “introspection”; columns ask what evidence each capacity must satisfy before it supports an introspective ascription. Meeting the first three criteria establishes a first-order route from internal state to report. Only the fourth distinguishes a metacognitive route. Each test must also control for known confounds and causal bypasses (Morris & Plunkett, 2025).

The capacities are ordered from coarse to fine: detecting that something occurred, locating it, identifying its content and source, and characterizing it relative to other states. Evidence for a coarser capacity does not establish a finer one. Control is included separately as a possible use of introspective information.

The asymmetry Each criterion excludes a different cheap explanation. Accuracy excludes guessing; grounding excludes input cues; internality excludes reading the model’s own output; metacognitive representation excludes a merely first-order detector. The first three establish causal state sensitivity, but only the fourth distinguishes metacognition.

Our empirical work

A case study on Llama-3.2-1B: 45 adapters trained to say which of ten sentences carried an injected concept vector, with Introspection Fine-Tuning (Hahami, Sinha and Jain) and our own method, DC-SFT, at three strengths of a KL anchor and five seeds, plus control adapters trained on random vectors, shuffled labels or no injection. The fine-tuned case is the one Singh et al. set aside.

Two main results

  1. Localization · grounding

    The injected state causes the report, but a note alone also sets it off.

    Patching out the injected state leaves only 8.7% and 13.5% of reports (pre-registered; Fig. 2). Yet with nothing injected, a note naming a sentence makes 42 of 45 adapters report an injection there (Fig. 3). The cause is real; the report is not grounded.

  2. Content-sensitive detection · grounding

    Concept vectors beat random ones, but not because of the concept.

    At 1σ, a concept vector is noticed on 31–44% of trials and a random direction on 10–11% (Fig. 5). The mean of the other concepts’ vectors is noticed on every trial, and adapters trained on random vectors show no concept edge. The edge comes from a direction all concept vectors share.

Together: training produces a readout that passes accuracy and internality, and fails grounding.

When would philosophers call it introspection?

Accuracy sufficesA robust pattern of reports
Interpretationism (Dennett): a robust pattern of accurate self-reports is all there is to find.
Self-interpretation from cuesAccurate, but from the text
Carruthers: we know our own thoughts this way too, by interpreting cues. He does not call it introspection in us either.
First three criteriaAccuracy, grounding, internality
Transparency (Evans, Byrne): we know our beliefs by looking outward, with no inner scan. Proust: feelings that guide without representing the state.
All four criteriaAdds metacognitive representation
Inner sense (Armstrong, Lycan) and higher-order thought (Rosenthal): introspection is a state about one’s own state.
Beyond the fourPhenomenal states required
Acquaintance views (Gertler, Chalmers): introspection is direct awareness of phenomenal states. Without them, there is nothing to introspect.
